# 📋 IRS Business Expense Receipt Requirements (Small Business Guide)

When claiming business deductions on your federal tax return (Schedule C, Form 1120, or Form 1065), the Internal Revenue Service (IRC) requires substantiation for expenses under **Internal Revenue Code (IRC) § 274** and **IRS Publication 463** (Travel, Gift, and Car Expenses).

This guide summarizes what legally constitutes a valid receipt and how digital/electronic receipts are treated during an audit.

---

## 1. The 5 Mandatory Elements of an IRS-Compliant Receipt

To withstand an IRS or state revenue audit, any receipt, invoice, or voucher must clearly document:

1. **Merchant / Vendor Identity**: The official business name, DBA, address, or tax ID of the provider.
2. **Transaction Date**: The exact date the transaction occurred (or rental period covered).
3. **Itemized Amount**: Numerical monetary figures broken down by line items (goods, services, tax).
4. **Description of Purchased Goods/Services**: Detailed line items (e.g., "12 Gallons Unleaded Gas", "3 Nights Lodging", "Client Strategy Dinner") rather than vague lump sums.
5. **Business Purpose (Written Record)**: While not always printed by the POS terminal, taxpayers must annotate the business purpose (e.g., "Client meeting with Acme Corp", "Job site travel").

---

## 2. The $75 Rule (IRC § 274 Recordkeeping Threshold)

Under **Treasury Regulation § 1.274-5(c)(2)(iii)**:
- **Receipts are mandatory for all lodging expenses**, regardless of dollar amount.
- **For other business expenses (meals, taxi fares, office supplies)**: A receipt is mandatory if the single expense exceeds **$75.00**.
- **Under $75**: While an itemized receipt is technically not required by statute for meals and local transportation under $75, taxpayers must still log the expense date, amount, location, and business purpose in a contemporaneous expense log.

> 💡 **Best Practice**: Always generate and save an electronic PDF receipt regardless of dollar amount to avoid scrutiny during state tax or IRS examinations.

---

## 3. IRS Revenue Procedure 97-22: Electronic & Digital Receipts

Can you use digital, HTML, or generated PDF receipts instead of paper slips?

**Yes.** Under **IRS Revenue Procedure 97-22**, electronic recordkeeping systems (including digital PDFs, scanned receipts, and computer-generated invoices) are fully acceptable if:
1. The electronic records are maintained in a secure, tamper-evident manner.
2. The system produces legible reproductions of all transaction details.
3. The taxpayer can readily retrieve and print the records upon IRS request.

Thermal paper slips from POS printers notoriously fade within 6 to 12 months. Converting your physical paper receipts into digital PDF records ensures readability over the statutory 3-to-7-year IRS audit retention window.
